# Experiential Training Module — Scheduling & Coordinating PT Referrals

*Ancillary Services Coordination · Workers' Compensation · "Intaketraining" workstream*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Audience** | New associates transitioning from SOP-based to experiential learning |
| **Focus** | Scheduling & coordinating **physical therapy (PT)** referrals for injured workers |
| **Duration** | 2 hours (120 min) |
| **Format** | Facilitated, interactive — role-play, decision drills, reflection (≤ 8–10 participants) |
| **Pre-req** | Completed the SOP read-through (this module *replaces* re-reading SOPs with *doing*) |

---

## Why this module exists — the shift
SOPs teach the *rules*. They don't build the *judgment* to apply them when a provider has no slot inside the SLA, an injured worker only speaks Spanish, or the authorization is missing. This module moves associates from **"know the SOP" → "make the call."** They do the real job on realistic cases, get immediate coaching, and reflect on *why* — so the rule sticks as judgment.

## Learning objectives — by the end, an associate can:
1. Read a PT referral and identify what's needed to coordinate it (provider, appointment, transport, interpreter, authorization).
2. **Schedule within the jurisdiction SLA**, choosing the right provider under real constraints.
3. Run a **professional, empathetic call** with an injured worker and with a PT clinic.
4. **Triage curveballs** — missing auth, SLA at risk, no-shows, provider cancellations — deciding *solve vs. escalate*.
5. Document and hand off a clean, defensible coordination record.

## Segment 1 · Open — from rules to judgment (10 min)
**Facilitator:** Open with a real failure, not a slide. *"A PT referral came in Friday at 4pm, Texas, 2-day SLA. By Monday it was still unscheduled — the worker called the adjuster, the adjuster called us. What went wrong isn't in the SOP. Today we practice the part the SOP can't teach."*

- **Ground rules:** it's safe to make the wrong call here; mistakes are the lesson; everyone role-plays.
- **Frame:** *"You already know the rules. Today you build the instincts."*

---

## Segment 2 · Anatomy of a PT referral — "Spot the gaps" drill (15 min)
Hand each associate a **printed PT referral packet** (de-identified). Give them **3 minutes**, then ask: *"What do you have, and what's missing to coordinate this?"*

**Sample referral (on the handout):**
> Injured worker: **Luis Romero**, DOI 04/18, lumbar strain (S33.5XXA). Referral: **PT, 2×/week × 6 weeks**, prescribing physician Dr. Patel. Carrier: Liberty Mutual WC, adjuster M. Boyd. Jurisdiction: **TX**. Auth ref: *(blank)*. Preferred language: *(blank)*. Transportation: *(blank)*. Worker phone: 555-0192.

**Debrief — the associate should surface:**
- ✅ Have: service (PT 2×/wk × 6wk), body part/dx, prescriber, carrier/adjuster, jurisdiction, worker phone.
- 🔴 **Hard gaps:** authorization reference (can't bill/confirm without it), appointment scheduling not started.
- 🟡 **Coordination gaps:** language unknown, transportation need unknown, no PT provider selected yet.
- ⏱ **SLA:** TX → **2 business days**. Clock is already running.

**Coaching point:** *"A referral is a starting point, not a finished job. Your value is everything that's blank."*

---

## Segment 3 · Role-play A — the PT clinic call (20 min)
**Setup:** pairs. One is the **Associate**, one is the **PT Clinic Scheduler**. 7 min role-play, swap, then group debrief. Use the card below.

### 🎭 Role-play card — Associate ↔ PT Clinic
**Associate's goal:** book the first available PT appointment **within the TX 2-day SLA**, confirm the clinic is in-network and accepts the carrier, and capture the appointment details.

**Scheduler script (the person playing the clinic):**
- *"We're booking about a week out for new WC patients."* ⟵ (the SLA conflict — associate must respond)
- If pushed: *"I might have a cancellation Thursday at 2 — but I'd need the authorization number first."*
- *"Do you know if Liberty Mutual is in our network? We don't take all WC plans."*
- *"Is the patient able to get here on their own?"*

**What "good" looks like (coaching rubric):**
- Leads with claim # + service, confirms network/carrier acceptance.
- Hears the "week out" and **negotiates / asks for cancellations** to hit the SLA — doesn't just accept it.
- Recognizes the **missing auth is a blocker** and flags it (doesn't promise what can't be confirmed).
- Captures: date/time, address, what the worker needs to bring.
- Doesn't over-commit; sets a callback to confirm once auth is in hand.

**Debrief questions:** *Did you hit the SLA? What did you do when they said "a week out"? What did you NOT have that you needed?*

---

## Segment 4 · Decision drill — provider selection under constraints (20 min)
Small groups. Present each scenario, give **90 seconds** to choose + justify, then debrief the trade-off. There's rarely one "right" answer — the goal is **reasoned judgment**.

**Drill 4.1 — Soonest vs. nearest.**
Worker in rural TX. Clinic A: in-network, **8 days out**, 6 miles away. Clinic B: in-network, **available tomorrow**, 38 miles away. SLA is 2 days.
- *Options:* (a) Book A (closer), (b) Book B (hits SLA) + arrange transport, (c) Escalate.
- **Debrief:** B + transport usually wins on SLA — but did you check the worker can travel 38 mi and whether transport is authorized? Distance is a *coordination* problem, not a reason to miss the SLA.

**Drill 4.2 — The network trap.**
The clinic with tomorrow's slot is **out-of-network** for this carrier.
- *Options:* (a) Book it anyway, (b) Find in-network even if slower, (c) Escalate for an out-of-network exception.
- **Debrief:** booking out-of-network = unbillable/denied work. In-network first; if none meets SLA → escalate for an exception, don't silently break the rule.

**Drill 4.3 — The language match.**
Worker is **Spanish-speaking**. The soonest clinic has no Spanish-speaking staff.
- *Options:* (a) Book + arrange an interpreter for the appointment, (b) Find a Spanish-speaking clinic (slower), (c) Book and "hope it works out."
- **Debrief:** (a) — coordinate the interpreter into the *same* appointment window. (c) is how no-shows and bad outcomes happen.

**Coaching point:** *"Scheduling isn't finding a slot — it's aligning the slot, the transport, the language, and the authorization into one working appointment."*

## Segment 5 · Role-play B — the injured-worker call (20 min)
**Setup:** pairs. **Associate ↔ Injured Worker.** 7 min, swap, debrief. This call is about **empathy + getting the coordination facts.**

### 🎭 Role-play card — Associate ↔ Injured Worker (Luis Romero)
**Associate's goal:** confirm the worker's availability, transportation need, and preferred language; explain the next step warmly and clearly; set expectations.

**Worker script (the person playing Luis):**
- Anxious / a little frustrated: *"I already told someone all this last week."* ⟵ (associate must de-escalate, not get defensive)
- *"I don't drive right now because of my back."* ⟵ (transportation need surfaces)
- *"My English isn't great — is that a problem?"* ⟵ (language need)
- *"When will I actually be seen? I'm in pain."*

**What "good" looks like:**
- Warm, names the worker, acknowledges the frustration ("I'm sorry you had to repeat that — I've got it now").
- **Surfaces transport + language naturally**, without an interrogation.
- Sets a **clear, honest expectation** ("I'm booking you within two business days; I'll call you back today once it's confirmed").
- Doesn't promise what isn't confirmed (auth pending).

**Debrief questions:** *How did you handle the frustration? Did you get transport + language without it feeling like a form? What did you promise — and could you keep it?*

---

## Segment 6 · Decision drill — the curveballs: solve vs. escalate (15 min)
Rapid-fire. Read each, 60 seconds: **"Do you solve it, or escalate — and why?"**

| # | Curveball | Solve or Escalate? |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Auth still missing at end of day 1, SLA is day 2 | **Solve first** — chase the adjuster (call + email) *now*; **escalate** if no response by mid-day 2 so the SLA breach is visible, not silent |
| 6.2 | Worker no-shows the first PT appointment | **Solve** — reschedule + confirm transport/reminder; log it; **escalate** if 2nd no-show (pattern) |
| 6.3 | PT clinic cancels day-of | **Solve** — rebook same/next day at the alternate in-network clinic; notify worker + adjuster |
| 6.4 | Clinical notes conflict (referral says lumbar, notes say cervical) | **Escalate** — don't guess a clinical detail; flag to the specialist with both documents |
| 6.5 | Worker requests a specific clinic that's out-of-network | **Escalate** — out-of-network exception is a decision above the associate |

**Coaching point:** *"Escalating isn't failing — guessing is. Escalate the clinical and the out-of-bounds; solve the logistics."*

---

## Segment 7 · Reflection & synthesis (10 min)
Two minutes silent writing on each, then share a few aloud:

1. **"What's one thing you did today that the SOP wouldn't have told you to do?"**
2. **"When you escalated, how did you decide it wasn't yours to solve?"**
3. **"What will you do differently on your first real PT referral tomorrow?"**

**Facilitator synthesis:** capture the group's answers on the wall and tie back to the objectives — *rules became judgment.*

---

## Segment 8 · Wrap — the one-case certification check (5 min)
Hand each associate a **fresh PT referral** (TX, missing auth, Spanish-speaking, no transport listed). On an index card, in 3 minutes, write:
- The **first 3 actions** they'd take, in order.
- **One thing they'd escalate** and why.
- The **SLA** and when the clock runs out.

**Pass = ** SLA correct (2 days), schedules in-network toward the SLA, surfaces auth + interpreter + transport, and escalates only the right thing. *This is the gate before live work.*
