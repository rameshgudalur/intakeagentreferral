# Playable Training Scenarios — PT Referral Scheduling & Coordination

*Companion to the 2-hour experiential module · Ancillary Services Coordination (Workers' Comp) · run these as live **demonstrations** (facilitator models the ideal first) then as **role-plays** (associates play).*

**How to use a card:** (1) Facilitator reads the **Setup** aloud. (2) **Demonstrate** — facilitator plays the Associate once, showing the model handling. (3) **Now you play** — pairs run it; the second person uses the *counterpart lines*. (4) **Debrief** with the teaching point.

**Cast key:** **A** = Associate (the trainee role) · **C** = PT Clinic scheduler · **W** = Injured worker · **ADJ** = Adjuster/case manager.

---

## Scenario 1 — The Clean Fast-Track *(warm-up · happy path)*
**Setup:** Referral for **Dana Cole**, knee sprain, PT 2×/wk × 4wk. Jurisdiction **CA (5-day SLA)**. Auth on file. Worker drives, English. In-network clinic 4 miles away with openings.
**The play (A ↔ C):**
- **A:** "Hi, this is [name] with Coastal coordination, scheduling a WC PT referral — claim WC-44120, Liberty Mutual. Are you in-network and do you have new-patient openings this week?"
- **C:** "Yes, we take Liberty. I can do Wednesday 10am or Friday 2pm."
- **A:** "Wednesday 10am — auth is on file, ref AUTH-7781. I'll confirm with the patient and send the order."
**Model handling:** leads with claim+carrier+network check; books inside SLA; cites auth; closes the loop with the worker.
**Teaching point:** even the easy one has a *sequence* — verify network → book → cite auth → confirm with worker. Build the habit on the easy case.

## Scenario 2 — The SLA Squeeze *(Texas 2-day)*
**Setup:** **Luis Romero**, lumbar strain, PT 2×/wk × 6wk. Jurisdiction **TX (2-day SLA)**. Auth on file. Clinic is the only in-network option nearby.
**The play (A ↔ C):**
- **C:** "We're booking about **a week out** for new WC patients." ⟵ *decision point*
- **A:** *(must not accept)* "This is a Texas WC referral with a two-business-day requirement — do you have any **cancellations** or an earlier slot, even a wait-list?"
- **C:** "…I might have a **Thursday 2pm** cancellation."
- **A:** "I'll take Thursday 2pm and confirm today."
**Model handling:** hears "a week out" and **negotiates to the SLA** (asks for cancellations/wait-list) instead of booking late; if truly nothing inside SLA → finds an alternate in-network clinic or escalates for an exception.
**Teaching point:** the SLA is a constraint you *work*, not a number you report missing. Always push for the earlier slot.

## Scenario 3 — The Missing Authorization *(solve → then escalate)*
**Setup:** **Marcus Bell**, shoulder, PT referral. Jurisdiction **FL (3-day)**. **Auth ref blank.** Clinic available tomorrow.
**The play (A ↔ C ↔ ADJ):**
- **C:** "I can hold tomorrow 9am, but I need the **authorization number** before the visit."
- **A → ADJ (call/email):** "Hi M. Boyd — referral for Marcus Bell, claim WC-55230, needs the PT auth reference to schedule; I have a slot tomorrow 9am holding. Can you send it today?"
- **ADJ:** *(no immediate response)* ⟵ *decision point: how long do you wait?*
**Model handling:** **solve first** — chase the auth *now* (call **and** email, hold the slot), set a deadline; if no auth by **mid-day on day 2**, **escalate** so the SLA risk is visible — never let it breach silently.
**Teaching point:** missing auth is a **hard block** — you can hold a slot, but don't confirm/bill without it. Solve the chase; escalate the breach risk.

## Scenario 4 — Transport + Language Combo *(multi-service coordination)*
**Setup:** **Rosa Méndez**, back injury, PT 2×/wk. **Spanish-speaking, not driving** (per injury). Jurisdiction **NJ (3-day)**. Soonest in-network clinic has no Spanish-speaking staff.
**The play (A ↔ W ↔ C):**
- **W (Spanish-speaking):** "Mi inglés no es bueno… ¿hay problema?" / "I don't drive right now."  ⟵ *two needs surface*
- **A:** must (a) book the clinic within SLA, (b) **arrange an interpreter into that same appointment window**, (c) **arrange mobility-appropriate transport** to/from.
**Model handling:** doesn't just book the slot — **aligns interpreter + transport to the same window**; confirms all three are set before telling the worker it's done.
**Teaching point:** scheduling = **aligning the slot, the language, and the transport into one working appointment.** Book the visit but forget the interpreter → guaranteed no-show.

## Scenario 5 — Day-Of Disruption *(no-show / cancellation recovery)*
**Setup:** Two variants — pick one.
- **5a (worker no-show):** Worker misses the first PT visit. ⟵ *decision point*
- **5b (clinic cancels):** PT clinic cancels day-of.
**The play:**
- **A (5a):** call the worker, find out why (transport? confusion?), **reschedule**, fix the root cause (confirm transport + a reminder), log it. *Second* no-show → escalate (pattern).
- **A (5b):** rebook same/next day at the **alternate in-network clinic**, notify worker **and** adjuster, log the change.
**Model handling:** recovers fast, fixes the *cause* not just the symptom, keeps everyone informed, documents.
**Teaching point:** disruptions are normal — the skill is the **recovery loop** (rebook → notify → log), and spotting a **pattern** (2nd no-show) worth escalating.

## Scenario 6 — The Clinical Conflict *(escalate — don't guess)*
**Setup:** **James Holloway**, post-op. Referral form says **lumbar**; the attached clinical notes say **cervical**. PT type/region differs between the two documents.
**The play (A, reviewing docs):**
- **A:** notices the **referral form and clinical notes disagree** on body region. ⟵ *decision point: pick one and proceed, or stop?*
**Model handling:** **stop and escalate** to a clinical specialist with **both documents** attached and the conflict flagged — do **not** schedule a PT type based on a guess.
**Teaching point:** logistics you solve; **clinical conflicts you escalate.** Guessing a clinical detail is the one thing that's never the associate's call.

---

## Scenario 7 — The Out-of-Network Request *(escalate for exception)*
**Setup:** **Priya Shah** insists on a specific clinic "my doctor recommended" — it's **out-of-network** for this carrier. An in-network clinic is available within SLA.
**The play (A ↔ W):**
- **W:** "I only want to go to [Clinic X]. My doctor said so." ⟵ *decision point*
- **A:** acknowledge, explain in-network options, and **escalate the OON request** for an exception decision — don't book OON unilaterally (unbillable/denial risk), don't dismiss the worker.
**Teaching point:** out-of-network is a **decision above the associate** — escalate the exception; keep the worker respected and informed while you do.

## Difficulty & sequencing
| # | Scenario | Skill | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clean fast-track | The coordination sequence | ★ |
| 2 | SLA squeeze (TX 2-day) | Schedule under SLA pressure | ★★ |
| 3 | Missing authorization | Solve-then-escalate; hard blocks | ★★ |
| 4 | Transport + language | Multi-service alignment | ★★★ |
| 5 | Day-of disruption | Recovery loop; pattern-spotting | ★★ |
| 6 | Clinical conflict | Escalation judgment | ★★ |
| 7 | Out-of-network request | Exception escalation + empathy | ★★★ |

**Run order for a 2-hour session:** demonstrate **1**, then play **2 → 3 → (break) → 4 → 5/6/7 rapid-fire.** Each card doubles as a **demo** (facilitator models) and a **role-play** (associates play) — and any of them can be fed to the live agent / simulator to compare the human's call to the agent's.
