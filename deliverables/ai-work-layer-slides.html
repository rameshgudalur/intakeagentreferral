Agentic AI-Led Automation
What This Is

Your operations runs on people making the same decisions, hundreds of times a day — answering referral calls, scheduling appointments, coordinating care handoffs, chasing missing information. Agentic AI takes that work and runs it automatically, with human in the loop, following the same rules your team follows today. The result is a faster, cleaner operation that does not ask your organization to change how it works.

One integrated Agentic AI work layer across your full operations — not a point solution for each process.

03 · Trust Building
Performance gates, not dates
Three mechanisms that build confidence before volume increases
Parallel Running
Agent output reviewed alongside manual processing during early ramp. Associates validate that AI decisions match their own — discrepancies update rules, not blame.
Audit Trail — No Black Box
Every decision cites the rule that fired. Associates can read, challenge, and understand the logic. The ICD-10 knowledge graph is 24 deterministic rules — not a model guess.
Performance Gates
Volume only increases when accuracy thresholds are met — not on a schedule. Associates see the data that earns each milestone. Trust is built on evidence, not mandate.
New Performance Metrics
Associates shift from cases/hour to exception accuracy and complex case resolution. A more skilled, more meaningful job — not a diminished one.

What Stays Human — Year 5
By Design, Not by Limitation
At full maturity, agentic AI handles ~47% of total operational effort. The other 53% stays human — by design, not by limitation. We are not a mindless automation machine. The agent is built to recognize what it should not decide, and to hand that moment to a person immediately.
1
Exception-Heavy Workflows
Complex ICD conflicts · disputed authorizations · multi-payer coordination · incomplete records with no resolution path.
Agent flags and routes. Human resolves and closes.
2
Provider & Patient Interactions
Clinical clarifications · sensitive patient conversations · care coordination requiring judgment · escalated case manager disputes.
Relationship and empathy cannot be automated.
3
Compliance-Sensitive Activities
Audit responses · regulatory filings · coverage determinations · documentation requiring human attestation.
Liability and sign-off cannot be delegated to an agent.
4
Urgency & Escalation
Same-day dispatch decisions · urgent equipment needs · provider demands requiring immediate authority · critical care gaps.
Speed plus authority — the agent escalates, a person decides.
5
Frustration & Disputes
Angry claimant calls · denied claim disputes · provider pushback on coding · situations where tone and de-escalation determine outcome.
Human presence is the resolution, not just part of it.
6
Low-Volume Edge Cases
High-complexity, low-frequency scenarios · novel claim types · situations outside the agent’s training pattern · new regulatory requirements.
Insufficient pattern data — agent flags, human handles, model learns.

Client Dependencies
What We Need From You
Four critical inputs that gate implementation speed  ·  Surface these at contract start, not go-live
1
SOPs & Processing Policies
Client must provide documented SOPs for each function in scope. If SOPs are informal or undocumented, a discovery workshop is required before design can begin.
SOP gaps are the #1 implementation delay cause — surface early.
2
Data Access
Client IT provisions read-only access to referral, authorization, and billing systems before Build phase begins.
No integration path is a blocker — API, HL7, JDBC, flat file, queue, or RPA — we meet you where your environment is.
3
Subject Matter Experts
Claims operations leads needed during design workshops, integration testing, and UAT. Estimated: 2–4 hours/week during design; full availability during UAT week.
Ops and IT leads sign off before build begins — no surprises at go-live.
4
Integration Decision
Write-back method must be decided before Build phase: API, message queue, or autonomous RPA for returning episode records to source systems.
Must be decided early — it determines connector architecture for every downstream function.

Implementation Considerations (1 of 3)
Technical Architecture
1
Deployment Model — On-Prem vs Cloud
Three paths: on-prem (no PHI leaves perimeter), cloud SaaS (hosted under BAA), or hybrid (deterministic on-prem, advanced inference in private cloud). Most WC/DME operators choose hybrid. Same agent stack, no re-architecture.
"We follow your data residency posture. Nothing leaves your perimeter without your sign-off."
2
Integration — Input to Agent
Referrals arrive via fax, voice, portal, or EDI. Connection methods: REST API, HL7, JDBC, flat file/SFTP, message queue, autonomous RPA. Read-only connectors — zero writes to source systems during intake.
"No integration path is a blocker. We meet you where your environment is."
3
Integration — Output from Agent
Episode records post back to dispatch, billing, and case management in expected format. Where API unavailable, agent drives autonomous process. No system modification, no IT project. Exceptions route to existing coordinator worklist.
"Your downstream systems see a completed episode record the same way a human coordinator would have produced it."
4
Data Sets
Agent connects to referral systems, authorization platforms, payer portals, and billing systems. HCPCS/ICD rules, state WC fee schedules, LCD guidelines in knowledge base. Raw fax/intake data cleaned and normalized before processing. No data warehouse project, no ETL program.
"Data quality has stalled most AI implementations in healthcare. We absorb that work — it's our responsibility, not yours."
